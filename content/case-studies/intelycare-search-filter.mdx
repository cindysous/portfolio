## Overview

IntelyCare connects per-diem nurses and nursing assistants with healthcare facilities needing flexible staffing. The platform's core value proposition is **shift discovery**: the ability for a nurse to quickly find and book shifts that fit their schedule, location, and specialty.

I joined as the **first design hire** and led a full redesign of the search and filter system — the feature through which every nurse starts their shift-finding journey.

## The Problem

### Shift discovery was failing at scale

IntelyCare's nursing community had grown to hundreds of thousands of clinicians across dozens of states. With scale came a fundamental problem: **the shift catalog had become too large to browse, but the search tools were too primitive to navigate**.

Key failure modes identified through data analysis and user research:

- **Empty results with no guidance** — searches for specific cities or shift types frequently returned zero results, with no suggested alternatives or proximity expansion
- **Filter state amnesia** — applied filters reset on back-navigation, forcing users to re-build their query on every session
- **Overlapping and contradictory filters** — specialty filters and credential filters used inconsistent logic, often producing fewer results than expected
- **No spatial awareness** — location-based search treated a 2-mile radius and a 50-mile radius with equal visual weight, with no map context
- **Mobile-hostile filter UX** — a collapsed filter panel with 12+ options was nearly unusable on mobile, requiring excessive taps to apply a basic query

### The business impact

Nurse drop-off from the search/filter step was measurable and costly. Each nurse that abandoned shift discovery represented a lost booking — and potentially a lost nurse, since IntelyCare competed with other per-diem platforms.

> "I know there are shifts near me. I just can't find them."
> — NurseReview feedback, recurring theme

## My Process

### Research: mapping the friction points

I conducted 8 moderated usability sessions with nurses across experience levels (new to platform, 6-month users, power users). Sessions included both think-aloud task scenarios and retrospective walkthroughs of their last booking session.

Key findings:
- **Power users had developed workarounds** — they'd set extremely broad filters, scroll through results, and mentally filter — because the filter system didn't support their actual mental model
- **New users gave up fastest** — empty state errors were especially damaging, as new nurses couldn't distinguish between "no shifts available" and "my filters are wrong"
- **Location was the most critical filter** — but it had the worst UX. Zip code search with no feedback was confusing for nurses in suburban/rural areas

### Redesigning the filter model

Rather than incremental refinement, I advocated for a **filter model redesign** based on three principles:

1. **Filters should narrow down a results set, not gate access to it** — starting with "all available shifts in your area" and filtering down, rather than requiring a fully-specified query to see any results
2. **State persistence across sessions** — saved filter preferences as a named default, with easy reset
3. **Transparent logic** — show nurses exactly why results changed as filters were applied, using plain-language feedback ("38 shifts match · removing specialty filter adds 124 more")

### The smart empty state

Empty states got a complete redesign. Rather than a flat "no results" message, the new empty state:
- Diagnosed which filter combination was most restrictive
- Offered a single-tap "relax this filter" action for the most constraining criteria
- Showed nearby results just outside current radius with distance context
- Preserved session context to avoid re-querying from scratch

### Mobile-first filter panel

The mobile filter experience was redesigned as a **bottom sheet** with:
- A persistent filter summary bar (showing active filters as chips, always visible)
- Grouped filter sections that could be individually collapsed
- Live result count update as filters were changed (before applying)
- A clear "Apply" + result count CTA at the bottom of the sheet

## Outcomes

After a phased rollout to a 15% user cohort, then full launch:

- **Search-to-booking conversion increased 27%** in the 90 days post-launch
- **Filter abandonment rate dropped from 34% to 19%** on mobile
- **Empty state exits decreased 61%** — nurses were finding results instead of leaving
- The bottom sheet filter pattern was adopted as the platform's standard pattern for all multi-criteria filtering surfaces
- The "smart filter suggestion" pattern was cited by the product team as a candidate for patent application

### What I learned

Designing for a marketplace with two-sided supply constraints is deeply different from single-sided product design. The search experience needs to serve both the nurse's preferences *and* the platform's need to make matches. The smart empty state was where these interests converged — it helped nurses find real shifts while preventing the platform from losing a booking opportunity.

The biggest lesson: **filter systems fail when they're designed as query builders rather than guidance systems**. Nurses aren't database engineers. They're people trying to find a good shift quickly.
