#### Context

Conduit's entity infrastructure was built for a narrower product. When the company pivoted to international money movement, the same data model was stretched to fit — creating structural constraints that didn't surface until the product scaled.

All entity types — senders, recipients, subsidiaries, customers, third parties — lived in one undifferentiated table. No classification. No hierarchy. Regulators and banking partners require clear entity separation; the system couldn't provide it. Resolving it required a coordinated overhaul across design, product, and engineering.

#### Goals

**Strengthen trust with providers and partners.** Compliance gaps risked losing banking partnerships and payment network access. Fiat on/off-ramps are core to Conduit's business — a defensible, auditable entity model protects those relationships long-term.

**Prevent disruption to client transactions.** Live integrations and active transactions couldn't tolerate a hard cutover. Phased migration and dedicated client support ensured continuity throughout.

**Build a compliant, scalable entity architecture.** The old model created a ceiling on growth and a floor of regulatory risk. Separating Clients, Customers, and Counterparties creates a clean, auditable foundation — and unlocks risk-based pricing, customer segmentation, and automated transaction limits.
